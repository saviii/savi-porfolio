title: "LLM ranking is a product problem, not a model problem"
publishedAt: "2026-10-16"
summary: "The hard part of using generative models to rank content isn't the model. It's deciding what 'good' means."
tags: ["AI", "ranking"]
---

When we started using generative models to score articles, the first instinct in the room was to argue about which model to use. It turned out to be the least important decision we made.

The real work was writing down, in plain language, what makes an article *worth someone's time*. Is it original reporting? Is it useful? Is it rage bait wearing a nice headline? Every attribute we asked the model to score was really a product decision in disguise.

A few things I believe now:

1. **Your prompt is a product spec.** If you can't explain the attribute to a new teammate, the model won't get it either.
2. **Scores are inputs, not answers.** The model's opinion is one signal among many. Ranking still needs to balance freshness, diversity, and personal relevance.
3. **Measure retention, not clicks.** Better content shows up later, in whether people come back.

The model is the easy part. Taste is the hard part.
